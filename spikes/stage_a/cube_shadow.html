What is the average shadow of a cube?

Tumble it at random, measure its shadow, average.

The average shadow of a cube

Light shines straight down. The shadow is the cube flattened onto the floor.

The average shadow of a cube

Face-on

A = s^2 = 1
Face-on, the shadow is a square: area 1 (side s = 1).

The average shadow of a cube

Corner-on

A = \sqrt{3} \approx 1.73
Tip it onto a corner: a hexagon, √3 times bigger.

Why the area changes

\text{shadow} = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\text{faces}} A_i\,|\cos\theta_i| Up faces cover the shadow once. Down faces cover it again, so halve it.
Each face casts a tilted copy of itself, area A·|cos θ|.

Tumble it at random

\langle |\cos\theta| \rangle = \tfrac{1}{2}
Over all directions |cos θ| averages to ½, so the mean → 1.5.
\langle\text{shadow}\rangle = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_i A_i
\langle\text{shadow}\rangle = \tfrac{1}{4}\,\text{surface area} = \tfrac{1}{4}\cdot 6 = 1.5

One quarter of the surface area.

Cauchy's formula

Sphere

\pi r^2 = \tfrac{1}{4}\cdot 4\pi r^2
True for every convex shape: average shadow = ¼ surface area.

Average shadow = ¼ × surface area
