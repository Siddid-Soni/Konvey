Drawn by the renderer as tiers of parts left to right joined by wires; a tier given as a list (`["client", "balancer", ["web1", "web2", "web3"]]`) is a column of parts, all wired to the tier before (a fan-out: a balancer and its servers, a queue and its workers). `holds` = a part's plain contents (rows of a table, ids in a store, a counter). `items` = things of their own that get built, sent, received: a request, a response with a body (`lines`), a job. An item always sits inside a part. Change its `in` in the next beat and it is sent: it runs along the wires as a small packet and opens in the new part. A message created and sent in the same beat is written in its receiver with `"from": "<sender>"`. `"layout": "ring"` puts one group of 3–8 peers on a circle, each wired to every other: peers that message each other directly (consensus servers such as Raft or Paxos, a gossip cluster, a token ring), e.g. `{"parts": ["client", ["s1", "s2", "s3", "s4", "s5"]], "layout": "ring"}`; a peer may then send to any peer. `"layout": "column"` stacks the places top to bottom (levels: registers, cache, memory, disk) and wires each to the next; `"grid"` lays out up to 9 places in rows. `"links": "none"` draws no wires: an item whose `in` changes jumps across in an arc (money between accounts, a card dealt to a player, a value dropped into a bucket); `"links": [["a", "b"], …]` draws only those wires.
