## Workstreams

### W1 — Story: example, hook, whys, payoff (Agents 1–2; #5–8, #13–16, #23–28)

Agent 1 (`schemas.KnowledgePack`, `prompts/shared/agent1-stage.md`):
- `examples_considered`: 3–4 candidate examples, each with what it makes visible and where it
  leads; `chosen` + reason. (If the request names an example: see decision D1.)
- `hook`: `{type, line, why}`, `type` from a menu taken from the 3b1b openings: stakes/context,
  real-world mystery, paradox, an object put in front of you, spoiler first, the narrator's own
  confusion, puzzle story, viewer prediction. Each with a one-line neutral example.
- `viewer_questions`: every "why?" a smart beginner would ask (why this number, why this rule,
  why not fewer, why does the formula hold, why is it called that, why should I care), each with
  `answer` and `shown_by` (`picture` | `derivation` | `counterexample`).
- `payoff`: `generalisation` (the toy case → the rule) and `surprise` (a use the viewer would
  not expect). `scope_exclusions` may no longer exclude these two.
- `trials` (for W5): when the claim is "always"/"never"/"on average", the exhaustive or random
  set of cases that shows it.

Agent 2 (`plan-narration.md`, `plan.md`, validators):
- Replace the single handshake script with 3 short scripts on neutral topics, each opening with
  a different hook type and a different sentence shape. Drop the stock phrases ("take a guess
  before…", "most people say…") as defaults; a prediction is one hook type among eight.
- Plan field `answers`: each `viewer_questions[i]` → the beat(s) that show its answer.
- Every `tex` object carries `derived_in` (the beats that build it) or `from` (picture parts it
  is assembled from, see W3). A formula with neither is rejected.
- A prediction gets a real pause: a held beat with the question on screen and `hold` ≥ 2 s,
  followed by the tempting wrong answer, then the correction.
- Validators: `check_plan_answers` (hard: every question placed, beats exist),
  `check_plan_formulas` (hard), `warn_hook_shape` (soft: "N things, M things, a number on each"
  opening; repeated stock phrases), `warn_long_hold` (soft: 3+ held lines in a row).

### W1b — The arc (user, 2026-10-19: "change the full narrative to how he approaches the problem")

Hooks were still the bare problem. 3b1b's ~130 caption openings almost never are: they say why
the question is worth the time (what it matters for, what it is really a lesson in, why it
seems impossible, why it confuses everyone), then the question. Whole videos (windmills,
hamming-codes, sphere-area, chessboard) follow one arc, now in the schema (`ARC_PARTS`):

why_care → problem → naive_attempt (where it falls short → sharper question) → promise →
exploration (small cases up to the troublesome one) → key_insight (hunch → made precise →
idea) → why_it_holds (and why it settles every case) → [objections, edge cases: `answers`] →
lesson → generalisation → surprise (a different situation) → closes_opening.

Agent 1 fills each part (`hook.why_care/problem`, `naive_attempt`, `promise`, `exploration`,
`insight`, `payoff.lesson/closes_opening`); Agent 2 places each on beats in `arc`;
`check_plan_arc` (hard): every part placed, why_care is S1 beat 0, order kept, closes_opening
in S3, first line not a list of counts. Runs: `out/story-arc/{haiku,muse,ds}/`.

### W1c — Story-first agents (user, 2026-10-19: "change the whole story narrative to 3b1b's so the facts, concepts and walkthrough align with that")

The in-planner hook still failed (answer leaked in paraphrase, no tension); a focused opening
prompt (`prompts/shared/opening.md`, eval in `scripts/opening_eval.py`) worked on ten of his own
topics. The user wants the story to drive the facts, so the agents change:

1. **Explorer** (tools): works the question as 3b1b would and returns findings with computed
   numbers, keyed to the arc steps (which are the same for every topic, so the explorer knows
   what to look for before any script exists); 2–3 candidates where a choice is open.
2. **Writer** (no tools): the opening (three drafts, kind by rule) and the whole spoken script
   along the arc, each line tagged with its step and finding; may ask the explorer once for
   missing numbers (`needs`).
3. **Visual planner**: today's Agent 2 without the writing: objects and picture changes per line.
4. **Director**: today's Agent 3.

First build and test 1–2 on the ten 3b1b topics against his scripts; then 3.

Status (2026-10-19 evening): Explorer + Writer built standalone, v1 → v3 (two acts with
deepening chapters); results, findings and next steps in `../handoff-2026-10-19-story-agents.md`.

### W2 — Composition: shots instead of slots (Agent 3 + layout; #9–12, #18–20)

Keep the renderer doing the geometry (weak models), but stop forcing one composition.
- Beat-level `shot`: `full` (the picture takes the stage, no heading), `question` (one large
  line top-centre over the picture, fades after its line), `close` (the camera on one part, see
  below), `compare` (two pictures side by side), `derive` (picture shrinks to one side, the
  equation is built beside it). Each is a renderer layout recipe; Agent 3 picks a shot, not
  coordinates. Titles become rare: `h2` only at a chapter change, with `fade="after"`.
- **2D camera** over the overlay: `<view focus="id>part" zoom="2">` and `<view pan="id">`.
  Implementation: overlay leaves are no longer `fix_in_frame`; they live in one `canvas`
  group, and the 2D camera is a transform on it interpolated like `lerp_camera` (text stroke
  kept constant, as `_steady_overlay` does for 3D). The 3D world keeps its camera.
- **Part addressing** for every display: `id>part` (a member group, a cell, a node, an edge, a
  bin, a drawing's shape). One hook per display, `parts(node) -> {name: Mobject}`; the
  collection's `from="group > sub"` becomes this shared syntax.
- **Attached readouts**: `<number of="id>part" side="top">` sits on the part and moves with it,
  replacing floating number widgets as the default (#20).
- **Annotation layer**: `<arrow from to>`, `<brace of label>`, `<ring of>`, and beat-level
  `focus="id>part,…"` that dims everything else (#18, #19). All by part reference.
- Prompt: delete rule 6 and the "Screen pattern"; add the shot list with one neutral example per
  shot; rewrite examples so most do not open with `<h2>`.

### W3 — Motion: things travel (#2, #17)

- **Cross-display travel**: a member/item named in one display can be written into another in
  the next beat (`from="drawer>socks"` on the target's `<li>`), and flies across: the source
  count drops, the target rises (the narration rule already asks for this; the engine cannot do
  it across ids today).
- **Equation from the picture**: `<tex from="2:bins>left, 3:row">` (symbol index : part) makes
  each listed symbol fly out of that part as the equation is written — the general form of
  `motion._fly` (3b1b's `TransformFromCopy`). Same for `text`.

### W4 — Recognisable objects and true colours (#1, #21)

- An in-repo icon set (vendored SVG, licence file; see D2), drawn single-colour so the theme
  tints it. `shape="icon:name"` in `collection`, `<icon name>` in `draw`. A short alias list
  (person, coin, card, ball, die, box, bird, sock, key, file, server…) so names stay general.
- A colour said in the voice must match: checker maps colour words in the `vo` (black, white,
  red…) to the objects named with them and rejects a role that contradicts (a "black" thing in
  `c1`). Dark things: `muted` fill with a light outline so they read on the dark theme.

### W5 — Simulations (#22, #29–32)

- A display hook `live(node, t)`: the hold plays it instead of standing still (`_hold` calls
  it; `_tumble` and `_descend` become the first two users).
- `<sim rule="…">`, option 3 from the conversation: built-in rules with parameters for weak
  models, a sandboxed custom rule (compute.py's restricted Python, `step(state, rng) → state`,
  objects in the sim's own coordinates, looks from W4 shapes) for strong ones. Seeded, so render
  and check agree.
- First built-in rules: `drop` (items through left/right choices into bins, stacking),
  `trials` (run every case or N random cases of a small experiment, flash each, tally — also
  the "all 8 orderings" montage), `walk` (random walk with trail), `particles` (wander + local
  rule: spreading, mixing), `collide` (1D bodies with a counter).
- Readouts bind to sim values (`<value bind="sim.count">`), as `tumble.n` does now.
- Checker runs the rule offline and compares its final numbers with Agent 1's.

## Pigeonhole storyboard (target for demos A–B)

1. Drawer of real socks (dark ones muted with a light outline, light ones ink); 3 fly out.
   "Must two match?" large, top-centre, fades. Pause.
2. Two bins appear under the drawer; sock 1 flies into black, sock 2 into white, sock 3 hovers
   over each bin, each lights "pair".
3. `trials`: all 8 orderings of 3 flash by, each pair ringed; beside it the 4 orderings of 2,
   the two escaping ones (BW, WB) ringed in another colour: 3 works, 2 does not.
4. Why: arrows from socks to bins; two arrows must share a bin. "At most 1 per bin → at most 2
   socks" assembled from the bins' counts.
5. Generalise: bins grow to 5 colours, socks needed slide to 6, the count attached to the row.
6. Name: socks morph into pigeons, bins into a coop.
7. Payoff (full canvas, new region via 2D pan): hair counts — more people in a big city than
   possible hair counts, so two share one.
